3 · Find the factors linked to these crashes

Every crash and road column is scanned and every value is listed. Rows in dark text are where your selected crash type happens more often than normal; grey rows are not over-represented (or have too few crashes).

How to read the table
Crash frequency – all crashes meeting the condition, split by the column you pick below (e.g. K / A / B / C / O).
Total #, % – all crashes meeting the condition, and their share of all crashes.
Group #, % – crashes of the group meeting the condition, and their share of the whole group.
× normal – how many times more common the group is under the condition than overall (1.00 = no difference, 2.00 = twice, 0.50 = half). ★ marks the strongest clear condition in each category (highest × normal with Prob. ≥ 95%) – use it to choose between several 100% rows.
Prob. – how sure we are the group is over-represented under the condition (Poisson test against the overall rate). ≥ 95% clear, ≥ 85% possible, lower = no real difference.
Tick a row to use it as a factor. Hover over any row for a plain-English sentence.

Different crash types usually have different factors. Analyze each type on its own and pick its factors, or use one set of factors for all selected types. Each choice keeps its own ticks.
